\paragraph{Fresh generations.}
We drew 30 held-out alerts by a seeded rule, stratified by E's frozen decision, of which 19 come from OWASP and 11 from the diagnostics, and re-ran L and E three times on each with new replicate IDs, which gave 180 fresh calls at temperature 0. L repeated its frozen decision in all 90 of its calls. E changed on one alert, the vulnerable \texttt{prefix\_confusion2}, which the frozen response kept but two of three fresh generations suppressed, and the gate accepted both suppressions as \texttt{semantic\_judgment\_remaining}. Even at temperature 0 decoding is not deterministic, and E's held-out count of two wrongful suppressions could as easily have been three, with the variation falling in the one category the gate cannot check.

\paragraph{Smaller model.}
With \texttt{qwen3.5:9b}, ordinary triage became more aggressive and more harmful, removing 23 of 33 false positives on OWASP while discarding 6 of 35 real vulnerabilities, and losing 8 of 23 on the pairs. E kept every real vulnerability on both corpora, although two of them survived on the pairs only through the fallbacks for a missing evidence record and an invalid reply. It added nothing over D, matching it on OWASP and removing one false positive fewer on the pairs.

The gate behaved differently with this model, since it rejected five correct E suppressions on OWASP and so lowered the FP removal of EC from 0.364 to 0.212. In every one of these cases the model cited line numbers where it had been asked for evidence IDs, so the value of the gate depends on the model following the citation protocol, and with the weaker model its rejections cost precision and recovered no vulnerability.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Table~\ref{tab:resources} lists the LLM cost of each held-out run, while the deterministic parts are negligible, since Semgrep and evidence extraction took under 2\,s per corpus. Latency depended mostly on the model, since the primary model answered a cold call in a median of 1.9\,s with a 90th percentile of 3.2\,s, whereas the small model needed 13.0\,s and 32.8\,s, so each LLM arm took between 2.5 and 13 minutes over the 68 OWASP alerts.

The table counts completion requests, which include schema repairs. The only retry that depended on answer content was the declared schema repair, which six requests needed in total, and this is why 68 OWASP alerts give 136 triage jobs but 137 requests. SoCLaaS also limited bursts, and the 171 transport retries logged across the four held-out runs repeated requests without depending on any answer.

The whole project, including development and both post-review corrections, used 874 uncached requests and 2.0M tokens, well within the planned caps of 1{,}500 requests and 12M tokens. SoCLaaS charges SoC users nothing and sets no quota, so these figures matter as shared compute and as a bound on what a larger study would need.
