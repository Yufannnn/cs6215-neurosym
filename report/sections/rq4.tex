\paragraph{Fresh generations.}
We drew 30 held-out alerts by a seeded rule, stratified by E's frozen decision (19 OWASP alerts, 11 diagnostic). For each, L and E were re-run three times with new replicate IDs, giving 180 genuinely fresh calls at temperature 0.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{L} repeated its frozen decision in all 90 calls.
\item \textbf{E} changed on one alert, \texttt{prefix\_confusion2} (vulnerable). The frozen response kept it, but 2 of 3 fresh generations suppressed it, and the gate accepted them as \texttt{semantic\_judgment\_remaining}.
\end{itemize}
Decoding at temperature 0 is therefore not deterministic. E's held-out count of two wrongful suppressions could as well have been three, and that variation falls exactly in the category the gate cannot check.

\paragraph{Smaller model.}
With \texttt{qwen3.5:9b} (thinking disabled), ordinary triage became more aggressive and more harmful. On OWASP, L removed 23 of 33 false positives but discarded 6 of 35 real vulnerabilities (TP retention 0.829). On the pairs, it lost 8 of 23 (0.652). E kept every real vulnerability on both corpora, but added nothing over D: identical on OWASP, and one removal fewer on the pairs (0.632 versus 0.684).

The gate behaved differently with this model. It rejected five correct E suppressions on OWASP, so EC's FP removal fell from 0.364 to 0.212. In every case the model cited line numbers instead of the evidence IDs it was asked for. The gate's value thus depends on the model following the citation protocol: a weaker model makes it lose precision, but never safety.
