Taint analyzers flag many path-traversal (CWE-22) warnings that turn out to be false alarms, and an LLM can be asked to dismiss them, although every wrongly dismissed warning becomes a missed vulnerability. We study this trade-off on identical warnings from Semgrep Community Edition and compare five configurations, which are the symbolic baseline, deterministic refinement by our own intraprocedural value analysis, ordinary LLM triage, LLM triage over structured program evidence with explicit obligations, and the same LLM replies filtered by a gate that checks the cited evidence against extracted facts. The subjects are 134 held-out cases from the OWASP Benchmark for Python and 24 held-out diagnostic program pairs that we constructed, evaluated with \texttt{qwen3-coder-next} and \texttt{qwen3.5:9b} under a protocol frozen before testing, together with a small case study of six real-world fix commits.

Deterministic refinement removed 36\% of false warnings on OWASP and 68\% on the pairs while keeping every labelled vulnerability. Ordinary LLM triage removed up to 79\% of false warnings but discarded up to 35\% of real vulnerabilities, and structured evidence cut those losses on the pairs from 7 to 2 with the primary model and from 8 to 0 with the smaller one. The gate never caught a wrong suppression and rejected one correct suppression on OWASP with the primary model and five with the smaller one, because it can check that a cited guard fits the dependency structure but cannot check that the guard confines the path. An independent review later found programs on which our extractor wrongly proved a warning safe, and after the correction every held-out evidence record, and with it every reported result, stayed the same. In the case study four targets fall within the task, and the baseline reported the documented defect in one of them, a fix-related flow in another, and nothing in the remaining two.
